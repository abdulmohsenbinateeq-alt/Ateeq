Quality audit

Ateeq, the tools are not the main problem. Three things are. The game has no single locked look. The map is built by code at runtime, so its lighting can never be baked. And AI is doing the two jobs it is worst at: placing furniture and judging what looks good. The well-reviewed games on Steam that use AI keep art direction, hero assets and lighting in human hands, and use AI where players don't look closely.

What the good ones have in common

  1. Hero visuals are made by people. AI sits where players don't look closely (ARC Raiders' robot movement) or AI is the mechanic itself (MIMESIS). None of them ships AI meshes as the face of the game.

  2. One consistent style beats asset detail. Lethal Company's low resolution, Schedule I's low poly and Clair Obscur's art direction all win because one look is applied to everything.

  3. The shortcut that works is library or scanned assets restyled by hand. ARC Raiders used photogrammetry, Bodycam used Megascans, and Clair Obscur reworked free Epic models.

  4. Visible AI art is punished. The worst reactions came from AI 2D art, AI voices, and AI placeholders that shipped by accident. Invisible AI (code, movement, tools) drew almost none.

  5. Your genre sells through clips of friends screaming, not fidelity. Your own GDD says graphics are not a success factor in this genre. The trailer needs a coherent, readable, scary world, not an AAA one.

High quality is not the same as high fidelity

"Realistic" is the most expensive look there is, and it exposes every flaw in AI meshes, missing bounce light and placement. The small teams in your genre that look great picked a look they could finish and held it everywhere. You can still go grounded and dirty, but it needs the lighting and consistency work below to read as real instead of clean and fake.

Section C

What's holding the look back

Ranked by how much each one drags down the image. Each plate shows what the sessions recorded, what the benchmarks or research do differently, and the fix.

01

Four different looks in the same frame

Highest impactRoot cause

What I found

  • The GDD says stylized PS2-era low-poly. On 10-24 the direction became "realistic".
  • World materials still use the PSX shader, props are realistic Dekogon and Megascans pieces, and monsters are Tripo mobile-preset meshes.
  • The dev session was told to ignore the Branding session, which is the only place a complete visual identity exists.
  • About 20 versions shipped, with a direction change at almost every one. No art bible or reference board for the 3D look.

What the benchmarks do

  • Lethal Company, Schedule I and Clair Obscur each lock one look and apply it to every asset.
  • Clair Obscur took free stock models and reworked them into its own style rather than mixing sources as they came.

Fix

  • Before another asset goes in, write a one-page art bible: palette, light temperatures, materials, texel density, triangle budgets, and 8 reference frames from your real hospital photos.
  • My recommendation: grounded, dirty realism seen through the 2012 phone camera. That filter is already your default camera, and it is in your brand. It can do for you what low resolution does for Lethal Company and pull mismatched assets into one image.
  • Retire the PSX shaders and move everything in frame to URP Lit or one Lit Shader Graph.
02

Lighting that can never be baked

Highest impactTechnical

What I found

  • The hospital is built by code each time the game starts, so everything is lit live by the lamp rig.
  • No baked bounce light, light probes or reflection probes. The PSX shader may not read Unity's baked lighting at all.
  • Your own feedback matches the symptom: very clean, not terrifying, not dirty.

Why it matters

  • Bounce light, soft shadows in corners and reflections on wet floors are what make a realistic scene read as real. Without them it looks flat and clean.
  • Unity cannot bake lighting at runtime, and probe volumes only cover geometry that exists when you bake.
  • Lethal Company and R.E.P.O. get away with live lighting because they are dark and heavily stylized. A realistic look does not.

Fix

  • Your map layout is locked, so generate it once in the editor and save it as a normal scene. Bake lightmaps for walls, floors and ceilings, Adaptive Probe Volumes for props, players and monsters, and one reflection probe per room.
  • Set lamps to Mixed. Keep only 3 to 6 live shadow lights (flashlights, flickering hero lamps). Baked light is also cheaper per frame, which helps the GTX 1070 target.
  • Bake two lighting scenarios, power on and blackout, and blend between them. That is your breaker-room mechanic from the GDD, for free.
  • Add volumetric fog (free URP package) and a build check that fails if the map changes after the bake.
03

AI placing the furniture and judging the result

High impactProcess

What I found

  • An AI-written placer picks coordinates for 2,110 pieces. Earlier placement failed visual review three times.
  • The latest ground-floor sample: sofas in a heap in the middle, bare shelves, and furniture cleaner than the rooms around it.
  • Claude judges the look from 1280×720 stills.

What the research says

  • Layout studies (2024 to 2026) find that language models are poor at exact coordinates. The better systems let the model state relations ("against the wall, facing the door") and a solver does the placing.
  • In SceneCritic, a vision-model judge agreed with human ratings of room layouts only 47 to 59% of the time. A rule-based checker agreed 83 to 94%.
  • On SpatiaLab, the best model scored about 55% on spatial reasoning against about 88% for people.

Fix

  • Have a person dress 20 to 30 small "story" groups once and save them as prefabs: an overturned gurney, an IV pole, scattered files and a drag mark. Tag each by where it sits: wall, corner, doorway or centre.
  • Claude writes the code that places those groups with wall-snap rules, then checks them in code: floating, overlap, facing, door clearance.
  • Use screenshots only to catch obvious faults. You judge the look at 1080p, in motion.
04

Monsters are the cheapest-looking part, and the riskiest

High impactCharacters

What I found

  • Tripo meshes on the game-mobile preset, Mixamo rigs, and every animation keyed by a Python walk-cycle script.
  • The $1,067 set of bought replacement monsters and motion capture is still waiting on your call.

What the benchmarks do

  • No genre hit shows a realistic creature up close. Their monsters read through one silhouette, one behaviour and one sound.
  • Script-made walk cycles move every joint on one rhythm: no weight shift, no follow-through, sliding feet. That reads as cheap instantly.
  • The monster is the most visible AI content in a horror trailer, and visible AI art is what Steam players punish.

Fix

  • Fix the retargeting properly: import as Humanoid, enforce the T-pose, apply scale. Then use real motion: MoCap Online packs for the humanoid monsters, AccuRIG 2 or Auto-Rig Pro for rigs, Cascadeur for clean-up, and Unity's Animation Rigging for the non-humanoid ones.
  • Take one hero monster to full quality first: clean topology at 15k to 30k triangles, rebaked textures with the fake shadows removed.
  • In the trailer, show it backlit, in fog, in fast cuts.
  • Confirm every Tripo mesh was made on a paid plan.
05

Bought assets used raw, then dirtied all at once

Medium impactAssets

What I found

  • Five or more sellers, each with its own texture density and material style.
  • Pivots up to 1.1 m off, a chair stored at 100× scale that became a 50 m collision wall, black ceiling tiles, and broken collision meshes giving 350 navigation errors.
  • One global dirt layer at "Heavy" is trying to make it all match.

What the benchmarks do

  • ARC Raiders, Bodycam and Clair Obscur all use library or scanned assets, but restyle them by hand into one material language.
  • Dirt in good environments is local and tells a story: grime at wall bases, scuffs at doors, stains under leaks.

Fix

  • Add an import gate in code that checks scale, pivot, triangle budget and texture density before anything enters a scene.
  • Move props onto a small shared material set: one or two trim sheets and a few tiling materials.
  • Replace the global dirt multiplier with decals and masks placed where damage actually is.
06

A slow loop with too many AI hands and no human artist

Medium impactMultiplies everything above

What I found

  • 77% of dev time is waiting. One map check takes 52.7 minutes.
  • Four AIs (Claude, Codex, Kimi, Gemini), with you copying prompts between them.
  • The monthly token cap was hit on 10-31. The 500 GB drive is filling with 116 GB of screenshots and 82 frozen copies.
  • Your friends' playtest feedback was set aside. There is no human artist anywhere in the pipeline.

What the benchmarks do

  • Every benchmark has at least one person who owns the look. Zeekerss made his own models and textures. Clair Obscur had four texture artists.
  • Content Warning and PEAK shipped in about a month by cutting scope, not by adding tools.

Fix

  • One builder (Claude Code) and one judge (you). Stop the multi-AI relay; every extra model adds its own taste and its own mistakes.
  • Polish a small beauty corner, not the whole map, so a check takes minutes instead of an hour.
  • Keep 3 frozen copies at most. Save review shots as JPG and delete them after review.
  • Put friends' feedback on a parked list instead of discarding it.
  • Hire one freelance environment artist for the trailer rooms.

A side note on شمقمق

MIMESIS (Krafton's ReLU Games, October 2025) is a 4-player co-op horror game built around monsters that copy players' voices and behaviour, and it sold over 2 million copies. It is not a visual issue, but your trailer hook should lean on what MIMESIS doesn't have: Najdi humour, the meme monsters, and the 2012 Irqah story.

Section E

The next 4 weeks

Aimed at your current goal, a stunning trailer map. The order matters: decide the look, light it, dress it, then add the monster and capture.

  1. Week 1Decide

    Lock the look and the shot list

    • One-page art bible and 8 reference frames from your real hospital photos.
    • A 60 to 75 second trailer shot list. It decides which 3 locations need finishing, for example the ER reception, one ward corridor and the nurses' station.
    • Decide to retire the PSX shaders.

    Done whenyou can hold up 8 frames and say "every shot looks like this".

  2. Week 2Light

    Bake the map

    • Generate the map in the editor and save it as a scene. Bake lightmaps, probe volumes and reflection probes.
    • Mixed lamps, 3 to 6 live shadow lights, volumetric fog, and power-on and blackout scenarios.
    • Profile on the 1070 class target before and after.

    Done whenthe empty corridor looks scary with no props in it.

  3. Week 3Dress

    Hand-dress the beauty corner

    • 20 to 30 story prefabs, made by you or the freelancer.
    • Claude places them with wall-snap rules and checks in code.
    • Asset import gate, shared materials, grime decals.

    Done wheneach of the 3 rooms answers "what happened here?" at a glance.

  4. Week 4Capture

    One hero monster, then the trailer

    • Take one monster to full quality: clean mesh, proper rig, real motion.
    • Add a "Trailer" quality level, record camera moves at 4K with Unity Recorder, and record a real 4-player session with voice.
    • Get the Steam capsule art made by a person from an in-engine render.

    Done whena friend who has never seen the game watches 30 seconds and asks where to wishlist it.

Section F

Steam and AI risk

  • Disclosure. Since the January 2026 update, Steam only asks about AI content that players see, hear or interact with. Claude Code needs no disclosure. Tripo meshes and any AI textures do. Write it plainly and early, for example: "Monster base meshes generated with Tripo AI, then rigged and animated by the developer. Environment art is licensed, human-made assets."
  • Tripo licence. Free-plan outputs are not licensed for commercial use. Confirm every mesh was made on a paid plan, or regenerate it.
  • Fab. The Personal licence gives the same rights as Professional and covers a commercial Steam game, as long as you were under $100k revenue when you bought. Megascans taken through Fab can be used in Unity. Buy the remaining asset stages before any publisher advance, because advances count toward that limit.
  • No AI in what players notice first. No AI 2D art (posters, Arabic signage, capsule art), no AI voices and no AI music.
  • Placeholders. Audit for AI placeholders before every public build, demo or trailer capture. That is how Clair Obscur, Anno 117 and The Alters got caught.
  • Awards and festivals. The Indie Game Awards and some festivals ban any generative AI at any stage. Check the rules before entering.
